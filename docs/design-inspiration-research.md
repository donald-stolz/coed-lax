# Landing page design research: pickup sports & nonprofit athletic clubs

Research pass over real, live landing/home pages from other pickup sport groups and nonprofit athletic clubs, done to find concrete visual reference material for `design-inspiration/` (which currently holds one screenshot). Scope was deliberately narrow: home/landing pages only — no signup flows, dashboards, payment systems, or league-management software. Each example was visited directly; roundup articles were only used to find candidates, not as the source of analysis.

For each example: primary/secondary CTAs, what's communicated above the fold and in the first few sections, and overall design style. A short synthesis closes out the doc.

## Cooldown Running — Austin chapter

**URL:** https://cooldownrunning.com/pages/austin

A social run club (multi-city, apparel-brand-adjacent) with an Austin-specific page nested in an otherwise ecommerce-style site.

- **CTAs:** "join the email list" (newsletter, appears twice), "start a cooldown" (nav link for starting a new chapter), plus standard ecommerce chrome ("Shop All," "Log in," "Check out") that has nothing to do with the run club itself.
- **Above the fold:** Day/time stated plainly — "Mondays at 7:00pm" — with location deliberately _not_ fixed: "meetup location is announced weekly on our instagram." Activity described in one line: "1-5 mile run or walk at any pace + drinks after." No cost mentioned anywhere (implicitly free). A named leader (Peyton Knight) is credited, which personalizes an otherwise anonymous group.
- **Style:** Minimalist, white background, large hero photo, clean sans-serif type, muted accent colors. Tone is casual and social-first ("good vibes," "drinks after") rather than athletic-performance framed.
- **Relevance to Coed Lax ATX:** The "day/time fixed, location announced weekly via Instagram" pattern is close to how Coed Lax ATX's rotating-field format would need to communicate itself. Worth noting the apparel-store framing doesn't fit a volunteer-run group with no merch, but the one-line "what happens + when + no fixed venue" copy block is directly transferable.

## Austin Front Runners

**URL:** https://www.austinfrontrunners.org/

Austin's LGBTQ+-inclusive running club — a genuine small nonprofit-adjacent community group, not a platform or brand.

- **CTAs:** "Get Tickets" (hero, event-specific), "View Events," "Get In Touch," "Learn More About Us →," "View Calendar" ("Ready to Join Us?" section), "Make a Donation," "Become a Member" and "Donate" (footer "Get Involved"). CTA hierarchy separates _come run with us_ (View Events/Calendar) from _support us_ (Donate/Membership) — two distinct funnels rather than one blended button.
- **Above the fold:** Tagline "Austin's LGBTQ+ Running Club, celebrating 28 years of running" establishes longevity/credibility immediately. Inclusivity messaging is explicit and early: "All paces and fitness levels welcome!" and "Whether you're training for a marathon or just starting your fitness journey, you'll find a welcoming group that runs at your pace." No schedule, cost, or specific location above the fold — those live one click deeper on the calendar.
- **Style:** Clean, minimalist, generous white space, centered layout, professional sans-serif. Tone is warm and belonging-focused rather than performance-focused ("vibrant community," "welcoming group").
- **Relevance to Coed Lax ATX:** The "who is this for" sentence pattern — pace/skill spectrum stated as a single reassuring sentence, right after the tagline — is exactly the shape of copy Coed Lax ATX needs for "never picked up a stick before" through "played in college." Splitting CTAs into a come-play funnel and a support-us funnel (even without asking for donations) is a clean pattern for separating "join a run" from "get involved" asks.

## Bridge Lacrosse

**URL:** https://bridgelacrosse.org/

A 501(c)(3) sports-based youth development nonprofit using lacrosse to serve under-resourced communities in North & Central Texas (including Austin).

- **CTAs:** "REGISTER," "VOLUNTEER," "DONATE" all sit in the top nav as equal-weight buttons. Mid-page: "Volunteer" ("Have a great skill set and want to work directly with us? Fill out our volunteer form!"), "Donate" ("We welcome financial gifts as well as in-kind support"), "BECOME A BRIDGE SUPPORTER" near the bottom. Social CTAs use a branded hashtag (#bridgefamily / #bridgefamilia).
- **Above the fold:** Mission-first, not schedule-first — large tagline "OUR MISSION IS TO USE LACROSSE TO BROADEN THE HORIZONS OF OUR COMMUNITY'S YOUTH," paired with "EQUAL ACCESS FOR OUR COMMUNITY'S YOUTH." A "Who We Are" section identifies the audience (urban, under-resourced youth, K-12 implied) before any operational detail. No pricing, schedule times, or specific locations appear on the homepage at all — cost-removal is implied by the mission statement rather than stated as a number.
- **Style:** Clean modern sans-serif, heavy ALL-CAPS for mission/vision headings, generous white space, single-column sections. Photos captioned "JUST A FEW FUN FACES OF THE BRIDGE FAMILY" — candid, unposed group shots rather than posed team photography. A Charity Navigator 4-star badge is displayed as a trust signal. Tone is aspirational and family-oriented: "IF YOU SHARE THAT BELIEF, WE WELCOME YOU TO BE A PART OF OUR FAMILY."
- **Relevance to Coed Lax ATX:** Good example of a lacrosse-specific nonprofit leading with _why_ (mission/access) rather than _logistics_ — the opposite ordering from Cooldown/Austin Front Runners. Since Coed Lax ATX is casual/for-fun rather than youth-development-mission-driven, this is useful mainly as a contrast: shows how much "mission voice" can crowd out practical "how do I show up" info, which is a trap to avoid.

## Mission Trails Lacrosse Club

**URL:** https://missiontrailslax.com/

A community-based nonprofit youth lacrosse program in San Diego (K-8, boys and girls).

- **CTAs:** "Learn about upcoming playing opportunities" (primary hero button), "Make a Donation" and "Let the Kids Play" (donation section), then a clear step-by-step onboarding sequence: "Register with USA Lacrosse" → "Find a Season or Clinic" → ... → "Download the TeamSnap App." Presenting onboarding as numbered steps rather than a single "sign up" button is distinctive.
- **Above the fold:** Tagline "Building Character Through Lacrosse." Audience stated directly ("K-8 age groups," boys and girls). Skill spectrum acknowledged ("Beginner clinics through competitive teams"). Cost is handled via a scholarship callout ("10+ scholarships awarded annually") rather than a price list — cost barrier is addressed without ever printing a dollar figure. Location stays deliberately vague at city-scale ("San Diego and surrounding neighborhoods"), no fixed address — same pattern this repo already uses for `areaServed`. A growth stat ("300+ participants and growing") is used as social proof.
- **Style:** Clean WordPress-theme sans-serif, navy accent color, moderate white space. Imagery is action photos of youth players in uniform plus championship team photos — posed-but-genuine, not stock photography. Tone is warm and inspirational: "Each of our coaches has a story for how lacrosse has positively changed their life."
- **Relevance to Coed Lax ATX:** The step-by-step onboarding pattern ("1. Register 2. Find a session 3. Download the app...") is a strong template for a "how do I show up" section, which Coed Lax ATX currently needs. The "scholarships available" cost framing is a reusable model for communicating no-cost-barrier without ever needing a real price list, even though Coed Lax ATX's situation (free/no fee) is simpler than an actual scholarship program.

## Houston Ultimate Community — pickup page

**URL:** https://houston-ultimate.com/pickup/

A nonprofit growing ultimate frisbee in Houston; this is specifically their pickup-games sub-page (not the org homepage), included because it's the closest analog to Coed Lax ATX's core "just show up and play" use case.

- **CTAs:** "PLAY NOW" and "DONATE" in the top nav (again, play/support split as with Austin Front Runners). "Click here for a full list" links out to a third-party pickup-game locator (pickupultimate.com) rather than listing games natively. "Check it out!" and "Want to Get Involved?" round out the page.
- **Above the fold:** Very little — the page functions as a gateway/referral to an external tool rather than a self-contained schedule. No cost, skill level, or specific locations are stated on the page itself.
- **Style:** Minimalist, text-light, logo-forward, an embedded map image stands in for real content. Simple imperative copy: "Check out local pickup in your area!"
- **Relevance to Coed Lax ATX:** Useful as a cautionary example rather than a model — offloading the actual "when/where do I show up" info to an external tool leaves the page feeling thin and the org more like a nav gateway than a welcoming front door. Coed Lax ATX should keep schedule/location info on-site (even if location changes weekly) rather than delegating to a third-party.

## Denver CityWide Adult Sports Leagues

**URL:** https://www.denvercitywidesports.org/

City-affiliated (Denver Parks & Rec) adult league operator, run at real scale (~300 teams/season) — included for contrast against small volunteer-run groups.

- **CTAs:** "REGISTER" (primary button below hero headline), "JOIN A TEAM" (secondary, directly below), four separate "Learn More" links under specific sport sections (Grass Volleyball, Kickball, Fall Softball, "Play for Denver"), sport-specific top nav (Basketball, Flag Football, Kickball, Pickleball, Soccer, Softball, Tennis, Volleyball), and even a clickable weather/cancellation phone line.
- **Above the fold:** Headline "DENVER'S HOME FOR RECREATIONAL SPORTS" plus "grab your crew or jump in solo" — explicitly covers both the "bring your team" and "show up alone" cases in one line. No pricing or schedule above the fold; multiple named locations (Wash Park, City Park, Sloan's Lake) are mentioned early as a system rather than one fixed venue. Skill division only surfaces for volleyball ("Recreational and intermediate").
- **Style:** Clean, spacious, high-quality action photography (real players mid-game, not stock), muted blue/gray palette, all-caps section headers, conversational energetic copy ("Come play like a kid again"). Notably foregrounds a "youth sports funding" civic narrative — i.e., a for-fun adult league justifying itself partly via community benefit.
- **Relevance to Coed Lax ATX:** "Grab your crew or jump in solo" is a directly reusable one-liner for Coed Lax ATX's "who is this for" messaging (solo drop-ins and friend groups both need to feel welcome). The multiple-named-locations-as-a-system pattern is a good model for communicating rotating fields without looking flaky or unreliable.

## South Austin Soccer (Meetup group page)

**URL:** https://www.meetup.com/southaustinsoccer/

Coed pickup soccer, run entirely on the Meetup platform rather than an independent site — included because Meetup-style pages are a realistic, low-effort format a volunteer-run group might actually use, and because it's the most information-dense example in this set.

- **CTAs:** Templated Meetup chrome ("Sign up," "Log in," "Start a new group," "Message" an organizer) plus group-specific: "See all" (events), "See all" (members), individual event links, and outbound links to a Facebook group and an unrelated real-estate business site run by the organizer.
- **Above the fold / event info:** Extremely concrete and operational — exact next date/time ("Sat, Aug 8 · 9:05 AM CDT," recurring weekly), exact address ("1100 Cumberland Rd, Austin, TX, US"), explicit cost statement ("no longer collecting money for games, unless they are reserved fields"), explicit skill-level reassurance ("all skill levels, whether you are in a league, haven't played in 10+ years"), format detail ("9v9 Pickup Co-ed," dropping to 7v7 if overcrowded), and a hard RSVP mechanic ("You must RSVP to play!!" / game auto-cancels below 14 RSVPs). Typical turnout is stated directly ("about 20-30 players per meetup").
- **Style:** Entirely template-driven (Meetup's standard layout: header, About/Events/Members/Photos/Discussions tabs, event cards) — essentially zero custom visual design, all differentiation comes from the text content and photos in event posts.
- **Relevance to Coed Lax ATX:** The single most operationally useful example for the "how do I show up" problem — RSVP thresholds, typical headcount, exact game-format contingency (what happens if turnout is high/low) are all things Coed Lax ATX likely needs to communicate but currently may not. Good proof that a group this casual doesn't need a custom-designed site to function — but also a reminder that Coed Lax ATX, being an actual custom Next.js site, should hold itself to a higher visual bar than a bare Meetup page while keeping this same density of concrete logistics.

## Volo Sports

**URL:** https://www.volosports.com/

A large, well-funded, multi-city adult social sports company (500,000+ players, 25+ sports) — included as one of the "well-designed larger org" contrast examples per the brief.

- **CTAs:** "Sign up" (top nav and mid-page), sport-category tiles (Soccer, Volleyball, Pickleball, etc.) that route into a city/sport selector, program-type links (Leagues, Daily Sports, Tournaments, Events, Private Rentals, Fitness Classes, Volunteering), "Become a volunteer" and "Learn more" (Volo Kids Foundation, its nonprofit arm), "Rent a court," app store badges.
- **Above the fold:** Headline "Your Next Game Starts Here" plus a scale stat ("Join over 500,000 players"). Notably, like several other examples, no pricing, schedule, or skill-level detail appears above the fold — the page prioritizes sport/city selection over logistics, deferring specifics until a city+sport is chosen.
- **Style:** Clean modern sans-serif, blue/amber/gray palette, colorful stylized sport icons, lifestyle photography, decorative SVG flourishes, carousel-driven modular sections. Tone is energetic and polished ("Real interaction, real activity, real fun, and real impact"), explicitly naming "something for every schedule, skill level" without detailing what that means yet.
- **Relevance to Coed Lax ATX:** Mostly useful as a scale/polish contrast rather than a direct template — Volo's page is solving a different problem (city/sport picker for a national platform) than Coed Lax ATX's single-city, single-sport, single-group site. The one transferable idea is the icon-driven, colorful-but-clean visual system, which reads as approachable rather than corporate despite the company's size.

## Link-in-bio format (Linktree / Beacons style)

A different design pattern worth considering: the single-scrolling-list-of-buttons format popularized by Linktree and Beacons, common for Instagram-first grassroots groups that don't run a full independent site.

### How the platforms themselves market it

**Linktree** (https://linktr.ee/, plus its Instagram-specific pitch at https://linktr.ee/link-in-bio/instagram): Positions itself as solving Instagram's one-link-in-bio limitation, letting "creators, businesses and artists connect their audiences to every piece of content they publish — websites, social profiles, stores, music, videos, products and more." The Instagram-specific page frames it as three problems solved: content discovery ("Help your followers discover, enjoy and support everything you have to offer"), monetization ("Sell your products, take service payments and add a Tip Jar to earn money"), and analytics ("track your clicks, measure click-through rates and analyze conversions"). Structurally, the marketing copy describes the output as a "one-page miniwebsite" with unlimited link buttons, built in "seconds" for "minimal time, no cost." Tone is casual, emoji-punctuated, and social-proof-heavy ("70M+ creators, musicians, influencers and brands"). Note: attempting to WebFetch the bare `linktr.ee/` marketing homepage returned only nav/feature text with no visual detail (the tool couldn't extract screenshot content from that render), so the structural description above leans on the Instagram-specific sub-page and the real example pages below, which render as actual content rather than marketing screenshots.

**Beacons** (https://beacons.ai/i/app-pages/link-in-bio — the root `beacons.ai/` domain returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch, so this sub-page was used instead): Headline "The most powerful Link in Bio," with the framing "Your link in bio should do more than link out." Beacons pitches itself further into creator-monetization territory than Linktree — the described page is built from "customizable blocks for links, video, music, images, embeds, and signups — stacked your way," with monetization blocks (digital product sales, e.g. a "$20 Interior Design 101 Ebook" shown as an example), affiliate links, email signup capture, and analytics/revenue dashboards. A custom domain is offered "for free." Tone mixes aspirational (a "$126K earnings" example) with accessible ("It's Free!").

Both platforms describe the same underlying structural template: a profile photo/avatar at top, a short bio line beneath it, then a vertical stack of large tap-friendly buttons/cards (one per link), optionally with thumbnail images, in an order the page owner controls. Everything is mobile-first — the format assumes the visitor arrived from a phone's Instagram bio tap, not a desktop search.

### Real examples: small pickup-sport groups using Linktree

Three real, currently-live Linktree pages run by small/independent sports groups (visited directly; not templates or previews):

**SoccerPickUpGames** — https://linktr.ee/SoccerPickUpGames

A grassroots pickup soccer group (Irvine/Orange County). Bio: "Hosting Quality Accessible PickUp Games Since 2014! Join the Fun and Community!" with a longer description: "We're a Local Grassroots Community Soccer Group Of Players & Friends Aiming to Enjoy Soccer Together while making a Fun, Safe, Quality Match As Accessible Possible!" Eight stacked links mix logistics (Instagram, a "WhatsApp Group & RSVP" link — this is the actual join/RSVP mechanism), content (game photos via Adobe Lightroom, YouTube game recordings, AI-analyzed game video via Veo for paying members), and one sponsor discount code. Established-since-2014 and "all ages and skill levels welcome" are both stated, but precise schedule (day/time) and exact locations are not clearly surfaced in the link labels themselves — a visitor has to tap through to WhatsApp or Instagram to get the actual "where/when." Style: casual, heavily emoji-punctuated (⚽️🔥🗺), white background, vertical stack of icon+text cards — the standard Linktree template with no unusual customization.

**Players Sports & Social Group (Chicago)** — https://linktr.ee/playerschicago

A larger adult social-sports operator (kickball, softball, volleyball, pickleball, cornhole, soccer, flag football, dodgeball, etc.). Bio: "Chicago's leading adult social sports organization!" This page demonstrates the format's ceiling for information density: 28 separate stacked links, mixing dated one-off events ("The Luau Weekend 🌺🏐 Sept 11th-13th," "Lazy Run 0.5K 🏃‍♀️ October 10th 2026 @ Lottie's Pub & Churchill Park"), sport-specific league sign-up links (one per sport), a "New to Players? Click here!" onboarding link aimed at newcomers, venue-specific links (Clark Street Pickleball, SPF Lincoln Park), corporate/private-event links, and even a "Work at Players" careers link. No pricing or skill-level info appears in any link label. Style: square thumbnail-image tiles rather than plain text buttons, still emoji-heavy and casual, but the sheer link count (28) starts to strain the one-scrolling-list format — finding one specific league requires scanning past unrelated ones.

**Pickup Play** — https://linktr.ee/pickupplay

Marketing page for a pickup-sports matchmaking app, not a specific game/group — included as a contrast. Bio: "Join the local pickup sports community. Download now to start organizing games with nearby players who are free to play when you are. Go play!" Only three real links (App Store, Google Play, website) plus Linktree's own footer chrome. No schedule, cost, location, sport, or skill-level info at all — the page's only job is to push an app download. Style: minimalist, blue/white, standard template.

### Comparison: link-in-bio vs. a traditional landing page, for a group like Coed Lax ATX

**Where link-in-bio wins:**

- Near-zero setup and maintenance — no code, no hosting, no build step. For a purely volunteer-run group, this is a real cost saving.
- Mobile-first by construction, which matches how most followers actually arrive (tapping the link in an Instagram bio).
- Forces radical prioritization — a flat list of big tap targets is hard to get "wrong" visually, unlike a from-scratch page design.
- Fast to update in-season (SoccerPickUpGames and Players Chicago both use it to post dated one-off events alongside evergreen links).

**Where it falls short, based on the real examples above:**

- **Weak "who is this for" narrative.** None of the three real examples devote space to a welcoming explanatory sentence the way Austin Front Runners or Denver CityWide do on their full sites — a link label ("WhatsApp Group & RSVP") has to carry logistics, not tone or reassurance. There's no room for a paragraph like "never played before? come anyway."
- **Logistics often live one tap deeper, not on the page itself.** SoccerPickUpGames' actual day/time/location require following through to WhatsApp or Instagram rather than being stated on the Linktree page — the opposite of the South Austin Soccer Meetup page, which puts exact time/address/RSVP-threshold directly in view.
- **Density scales badly.** Players Chicago's 28-link page shows what happens once a group covers multiple sports/venues/events — the flat-list format has no hierarchy beyond top-to-bottom order, so it degrades into a long scroll rather than organized sections.
- **Much weaker for SEO/AEO/GEO.** This repo's `docs/seo.md` strategy (structured data, sitemap, `llms.txt`, targeting search phrases like "pickup lacrosse Austin") has no equivalent on a Linktree/Beacons page — those platforms own the domain, control the metadata, and give no room for the FAQ-style structured content that answer engines and AI crawlers pull from. A group relying solely on link-in-bio is effectively invisible to search/AI discovery outside of people who already follow the Instagram account.
- **No visual room for brand/tone.** Beacons' own pitch leans hard into creator monetization (Tip Jar, product sales, affiliate links) which is irrelevant/off-tone for a free coed pickup group — adapting either tool means suppressing most of what the platform is actually built to highlight.

**Verdict for Coed Lax ATX:** Link-in-bio is a reasonable _minimum-viable_ format for a group that only has an Instagram account and nothing else, and the "WhatsApp Group & RSVP"-as-single-CTA pattern from SoccerPickUpGames is worth keeping in mind if Coed Lax ATX's actual real-time coordination lives in a group chat rather than on the site. But given Coed Lax ATX already has a real Next.js site with SEO/AEO/GEO infrastructure in place (per `docs/seo.md`), the traditional landing page remains the right primary format — link-in-bio's advantages (zero-maintenance, mobile-first) are already available another way (a well-built single homepage is just as fast to scan on mobile), while its disadvantages (no SEO surface, no room for "who is this for" tone, degrades under information density) are real regressions from what's already built. This doesn't change the screenshot shortlist below; it's a reason to _not_ pursue this format further rather than a design source to pull screenshots from.

## Synthesis

**Patterns that recur across the good examples:**

1. **"Who is this for" is answered in one sentence, early, before any operational detail.** Austin Front Runners ("all paces and fitness levels welcome"), Denver CityWide ("grab your crew or jump in solo"), and South Austin Soccer ("all skill levels, whether you are in a league, haven't played in 10+ years") all do this within the first screen or first paragraph. This is the single highest-priority copy pattern to borrow.
2. **Cost-barrier removal is usually implied, not itemized.** None of the community-run examples print a price list on the homepage; free/low-cost groups either say nothing (Cooldown, South Austin Soccer's "no longer collecting money") or route cost concerns through a scholarship/access narrative (Mission Trails, Bridge Lacrosse). A blunt "it's free" statement (as South Austin Soccer effectively gives) is simpler and probably right for Coed Lax ATX.
3. **Rotating/no-fixed-location groups treat location as a system, not a gap.** Cooldown ("announced weekly on Instagram"), Denver CityWide (named list of parks), and Mission Trails (city-level `areaServed`-style vagueness) all turn "we don't have one field" into a stated, confident pattern rather than a missing field. This directly validates the current `areaServed: "Austin, TX"` decision already documented in `docs/seo.md`.
4. **CTAs split into two funnels: "come play" vs. "support/get involved."** Austin Front Runners and Bridge Lacrosse both keep a play-with-us action (View Events/Calendar, Register) visually separate from a support action (Donate/Volunteer/Become a Supporter) rather than one ambiguous button.
5. **The most operationally concrete example (South Austin Soccer) is also the least visually designed one.** Concrete logistics (exact time, address, RSVP threshold, format contingency) matter more to a pickup group's usefulness than production values — a reminder not to let visual polish crowd out the "how do I actually show up" details.
6. **Mission-first nonprofit framing (Bridge Lacrosse) can bury logistics.** Their homepage has no schedule or location at all — a useful negative example given Coed Lax ATX is casual/for-fun rather than a youth-development mission, so it should lean toward the logistics-forward end of this spectrum, not the mission-forward end.

**Shortlist for actual screenshots into `design-inspiration/`:**

1. **Austin Front Runners** (https://www.austinfrontrunners.org/) — closest match in spirit (small, volunteer/community-run, Austin-based, "all paces welcome" messaging, clean minimalist layout) and the best model for the CTA split between playing and supporting.
2. **Mission Trails Lacrosse Club** (https://missiontrailslax.com/) — same sport, best example of the step-by-step "how do I show up" onboarding sequence and scholarship-style cost-barrier framing, plus a warm/candid photo style worth referencing.
3. **Denver CityWide Adult Sports Leagues** (https://www.denvercitywidesports.org/) — best "grab your crew or jump in solo" one-liner and best example of presenting multiple rotating locations confidently; also useful as the one higher-production-value/larger-org example for contrast, per the brief.

Runner-up worth a screenshot if a fourth is wanted: **South Austin Soccer's Meetup page** (https://www.meetup.com/southaustinsoccer/), purely for the density of concrete logistics copy (RSVP threshold, format contingency, typical headcount) even though its visual design is entirely templated.
